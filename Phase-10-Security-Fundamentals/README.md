# Phase 10 — Security Fundamentals

## Objective
Review foundational cybersecurity principles and connect them to practical lab work.

## Topics Covered
- Confidentiality, integrity, and availability (CIA triad)
- Authentication and authorization
- Least privilege
- Defense in depth
- Risk, threats, vulnerabilities, and controls
- Security updates and secure configuration

## Activities
- Identify examples of confidentiality, integrity, and availability in a computer lab.
- Review why accounts should have only the permissions they need.
- Consider how updates, firewalls, and backups reduce risk.
- Connect network scanning and packet analysis to security assessment.

## Analysis and Findings
- The lab's networking and scanning exercises demonstrate that systems expose information through their configuration and network behavior.
- A reachable host or open port is not automatically a vulnerability; risk depends on the service, configuration, exposure, and business need.
- Firewalls and least-privilege access can reduce unnecessary exposure.
- No formal risk score or vulnerability severity rating was recorded for this phase.

## What I Learned
Security is a combination of people, processes, and technology. No single control prevents every problem, so layered defenses and ongoing review are important.

## Cybersecurity Relevance
These principles are used by security analysts when prioritizing findings, explaining risk, and recommending practical safeguards.
